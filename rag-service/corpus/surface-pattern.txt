The surface pattern

WHAT THIS PATTERN MEANS

Your errors were phonetically plausible. Read them aloud and they give the right word - the
sounds survived, but the conventional spelling did not.

This is the signature associated with weak stored word forms rather than weak sound
processing. Put plainly: you can hear the word perfectly well. What is not yet reliable is
the stored picture of how it is written.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE IN WRITING

Spelled the way it sounds. "enough" becomes "enuf".

Silent letters dropped. "whistle" becomes "wisle".

The right sound written with the wrong vowel team. "school" becomes "skool".

Doubled consonants added where they do not belong, or dropped where they do.

Homophones swapped - their for there, to for too. These count towards this pattern as well,
because choosing between them depends on the stored word rather than on the sound. A
spell-checker will never flag them, since nothing is actually misspelled.

WHAT HELPS

The practice that suits this pattern is orthographic pattern work: building the stored
picture of the word.

Word families and rimes. Learn "light", "night", "sight" and "fight" together as a group
that shares a pattern, rather than as four unrelated words.

High-frequency irregular words with visual recall. Use look-cover-write-check. Look at the
word, cover it, write it from memory, then uncover and check it letter by letter. The
checking step is the one that does the work - do not skip it.

Homophone pairs in context. Learn the pair together, with a sentence that fixes which is
which. Then practise choosing between them inside real sentences, not in isolation.

Morpheme-based vocabulary work. Learn the meaningful parts that words are built from, so
that spelling can follow meaning rather than sound alone.

HOW TO PRACTISE

Read your writing back slowly, looking at the words rather than listening to them. Your ear
will approve spellings that your eye should catch, so reading aloud is the wrong check for
this pattern.

When you fix a word, look at the corrected form for a few seconds before moving on. You are
trying to store a picture, and that takes a moment of attention.
